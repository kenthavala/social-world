The following document discusses the rollout of a congestion pricing scheme in the city centre.

In March 2024 the city council approved a congestion charge of 15 pounds per day for vehicles entering the central zone between 7am and 7pm. The policy followed a year of consultation, public consultation events, and a commissioned study of traffic patterns.

The central zone covers approximately 20 square kilometres. The charge applies to petrol, diesel and electric vehicles. Emergency vehicles and public transport are exempt.

Local business groups raised concerns about access to the city centre for deliveries and for customers without cars. Retail associations argued that the charge would reduce footfall during the evening economy.

Supporters argued that the charge would reduce congestion on main arterial roads by an estimated 18 percent, and that the revenue could be reinvested in public transport.

Critics noted that the public believed the charge would be paid by drivers who rarely used the affected roads, and that the low-traffic neighbourhoods programme had already reduced traffic in some residential areas.

A public consultation in April 2024 found that a majority of respondents supported the principle of congestion charging, though support varied by group. A follow-up consultation in August 2024 showed shifting opinion after the announcement.

The policy was challenged in the high court by an alliance of environmental groups, who argued the consultation process was inadequate. The court upheld the charge in September 2024.

Further amendments in October 2024 exempt small businesses with fewer than 10 employees from the charge.

This report examines how residents, commuters, business owners and elected officials might respond to the final version of the policy.
